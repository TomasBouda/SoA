The game

For anyone who has not met it

What it is, who you are, where the thirteen missions go and who is waiting there - from the game's own texts, with nothing said about how it ends.

Squad tactics in real time, 2002, from Silver Style in Berlin: a handful of soldiers you know by name, a few vehicles you have to find and keep running, and a campaign of thirteen missions on 1200-metre maps of a Russia nobody has been to for ten years.

In November 2004 an engineered virus, SGDS, got out. Cities went under quarantine, Paris was bombed by its own government, and the world came apart. Six people at the Russian base Kalinina - four Russians, an American, a German - blew up the only road into their valley and went down into the bunker. That was ten years ago. Now the rations are the same, the radio has been silent for years, and they have decided they would rather die in fresh air than live another day underground. The campaign starts at the bunker door.

Between missions you are back at the base: the hangar and the workshop for the vehicles, the hospital for the wounded, a trader who comes by, the equipment screen where every soldier is armed piece by piece. What you bring home from a mission is what you have for the next one - the T-55 you captured, the Hind you found in a barn, the sniper you talked into joining.

The game's own text calls the world after the virus the New Times, and everything before it the Old Times.

The six in the bunker

  • Roman Willstein - German first lieutenant, the leader and the voice of the intro; a badly healed leg keeps him at the base.
  • David Reaves - US Army sergeant from Philadelphia; leads the squad in the field.
  • Boris Kerkowitsch - Russian lance sergeant, once head of the base guard; explosives.
  • Prof. Sergej Petrow - geneticist from Kiev, ran the base's research. The one who said to stay down.
  • Prof. Vigo Jolinzky - chemist, in a wheelchair since 2007, black humour intact; your voice on the radio.
  • Sorana Sorgarowa - Petrow's assistant, the medic.

The campaign, and where it forks

Two choices change what comes next. In mission 4 you pick one of two gangs to fight for, and that decides which of two mirrored roads you take to mission 7; there the paths meet again. In mission 7 you choose a side once more, and this time the roads do not meet: two last missions, two endings. Nothing said here about which is which.

Who is out there

As the game's own notes panel at the base describes them - the entry for each appears once you have met them.

Trade

Seekers

The traders of the New Times. They pick the ruins - and the battlefields - clean and sell what they find, each in a territory his guild assigns him. Nobody shoots a Seeker: the gangs need what he brings.

Civilians

Survivors

Villages and free towns, run by whoever could work with their hands before the disaster. Loyal to each other, and handy enough with a rifle after years of the gangs.

Gang

Slingers

Thugs and thieves who think they own the wasteland. They rob and kidnap villagers, have no training and little equipment, and run when a real fight starts.

Gang

Claws

Alcohol, drugs and territory, held with the cruelty of the criminals most of them were. No military schooling, but years of practice with every weapon still around - and as well armed as anyone.

Gang

TFR

Born of a Russian special unit and still fighting like one - tactically, with heavy equipment - and otherwise a gang of contract killers that wipes out whole villages. At war with the Claws.

Science

NOAH

Scientists and soldiers in hidden laboratories, working on a vaccine and on how 2004 happened. Only their Seekers know where they are. Well armed for people who say they only do research.

Sect

COTUC

The Church Of The Undying Child, an obscure 1980s sect that came through the virus almost untouched and is now one of the largest armies there is, with weapons nobody else has. Suspected, for that reason, of having had a hand in 2004.

Sect

Undead Knights

Half man, half machine: COTUC's cyborg soldiers, armoured against ordinary ammunition, weapons built into their bodies, loyal past doubt.
